Halloween Candy Board

Every empty doorstep leaves you a candy. Here's where they go.

Overview

During the community's Halloween event, you knock on doors in Discord with /knock. When nobody's home, you don't leave empty-handed: the house leaves a candy on the step, and that candy is yours to keep.

Your candy board on CharDB is where they live. Stick them on, pile them up, set the scene behind them and the light above them, give the board a name, and share it with anyone.

Finding Your Board

While the event is on, the community's sidebar has a Candy Board link. It takes you to your own board, which is made the first time you visit, so it has a link to share before anything is on it.

Your candies come from your Discord account, so your board needs Discord linked to your CharDB account. If it isn't, the page asks you to link it first.

Your board's address is private until you share it. It's a random link, not your username, so nobody can look boards up by name. Only people you give it to can find it.
2

Sticking Candies On

Under the board, Your candies holds every candy that isn't on the board yet. Candies of the same kind share a tile, with a badge saying how many you have. The tiles are in name order, so a kind stays in the same place however many you use.

  • Tap a candy to stick it in the middle of the board.
  • Drag a candy onto the board to stick it exactly where you drop it.

A candy taken off the board goes back to your tray, ready to stick on again.

The tray: one tile per kind, with how many you have
3

Arranging Your Board

Click a candy on the board to pick it up. A dashed outline appears around it, with a handle on each corner and one above it.

  • Drag the candy to move it.
  • Drag a corner to make it bigger or smaller.
  • Drag the top handle to turn it. Hold Shift to turn in steps of 15°.

Candies can pile on top of each other. Clicking the see-through edge of a sticker reaches whatever is beneath it, and clicking a candy you've already picked picks the one under it instead, so even the bottom of a pile is in reach. Click anywhere off the board to put the candy down.

The toolbar under the board does the same with sliders, and more:

Control What it does
Rotate and Scale sliders Turn the candy, or change its size
Bring to front, Bring forward Move it to the top of the pile, or one place up
Send backward, Send to back Move it one place down, or to the bottom of the pile
✕ Remove sticker Put it back in your tray
A candy picked up, with its handles and the toolbar

The keyboard works on the picked-up candy too:

Keys What they do
← ↑ → ↓ Nudge it; with Shift, five times as far
[ ] Turn it 15° either way
- = Make it smaller or bigger
Delete or Backspace Put it back in your tray
Everything saves by itself. Beside the buttons at the top, the board says Saving… while a change is on its way and Saved once it's arrived. If a save can't get through, your changes wait and a Retry button appears. Changes still waiting when you leave the page are sent as you go.

To start over, Clear board puts every candy back in your tray, once you've confirmed.

4

Choosing a Background

A new board starts on graph paper. Background opens a choice of full pictures and repeating tiles, with a large preview at the top that shows your choice under the board's own light, just as it will look.

A busy picture can crowd your candies, so four controls let it sit back:

  • Saturation: how much of its colour it keeps. Every background starts a little muted, at 90%.
  • Dim: darkens it, by up to 80%.
  • Tint: recolours it toward one hue, keeping its light and dark. Pick none, one of six Halloween colours (Pumpkin, Witch purple, Slime green, Midnight, Blood red and Bone), or any colour from the rainbow swatch.
  • Tint strength: how far toward that colour it goes.

Nothing changes on the board until you press Use background. Cancel leaves it as it was.

5

Setting the Light

Every candy casts a shadow. Lighting opens a small panel for the light that casts them, and the board follows as you move it.

  • The circle is your board seen from above. Drag the sun to move the light, or use the arrow keys (Home puts it overhead). Shadows fall away from it.
  • Height, the upright slider: a low light throws long shadows, a high one short shadows.
  • Intensity: how dark and sharp the shadows are.
  • Colour and Colour strength: light the whole board in a colour, from white to a warm pumpkin glow or an eerie green.
  • Spotlight: darkens the board outside a pool of light. Cone widens or narrows the beam.

A spotlight shines across the board, so its pool lands on the far side from the sun, stretched along the beam the lower the light hangs. A dashed outline on the circle shows where it lands. The more intense the light, the brighter the pool glows.

The reset button at the corner of the circle brings back the default light.

The Lighting panel, with a spotlight on

Naming Your Board

Name lets you give your board a name, built from Halloween words: a first word, a joiner if you like, and a last word. Moonlit von Pumpkin, Spooky Cauldron, Crisp under the Toadstool.

  • Pick a word from each column. The name appears large at the top as you go.
  • Stuck for ideas? Shuffle rolls a random name.
  • Use name saves it. Remove name takes it away again.

Your board's name becomes its title, for you and for everyone you share it with, and the title of the browser tab.

Building a name, word by word
7

The Nameplate

Once your board has a name, a nameplate waits at the start of your tray: an orange ribbon banner with your board's name on it. Stick it on, move it, turn it and size it just like a candy. It even casts a shadow.

  • Each board has one nameplate.
  • Rename your board, and the banner changes with it.
  • Remove the name, and the banner steps off the board. Name it again, and the banner is back exactly where you left it.

The banner across the top of the board at the start of this page is a nameplate.

8

Sharing Your Board

Copy link copies your board's address. Anyone you give it to sees your board as you made it: its name, its candies, its background and its light. They don't need to be signed in.

Visitors can only look. There is no tray, no toolbar and no buttons for them, and the title tells them whose board it is.

Show off your haul. Every candy on a board came from a door that went unanswered. A full board is the record of a lot of knocking.
