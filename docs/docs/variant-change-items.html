Variant Change Items

Moving a character from one variant to another, and what it costs

0

What one of these is

A variant change item moves one of your characters from one variant to another. A community sells or awards one; the member redeems it on a character they own; the character is the new variant from that moment.

It is an ordinary redeemable item whose type has been told which variant it grants and which characters it can be spent on — so it can be traded, granted, sold in the shop, and it has a provenance page like anything else.

Holding it is the permission. Changing a character’s variant is otherwise staff-only — a member who can edit their own character’s registry still cannot touch its variant, because that is exactly what these items are sold for. Redeeming one is not an exception carved into that rule; it is a separate route that exists only while you hold the item.

These are the “upgrade tickets” most communities mean. But CharDB deliberately does not call them that, and neither should you assume it knows what an upgrade is. A variant has a name, not a rank — nothing anywhere in CharDB knows that Legendary is worth more than Common. So the same feature configures a promotion, a demotion, or a sideways swap between two variants of the same tier, and the app cannot tell the difference. Name your item Rare Upgrade Ticket and that is what your members see.

1

The part that catches people out

There is no review, and there is no undo. An MYO slot and an edit kit both put something in front of staff, and refusing either hands the item back. This does not. The moment the member confirms, the item is gone and the character is the new variant — and the only way back is another item pointing the other way.

That is deliberate rather than an oversight. A review would have to be able to undo a variant change on refusal, which means either taking back something the member paid for or minting the item a second time. Your community already decided what this item may do when you configured it; the redemption is the member exercising that, not asking for it.

The practical consequence for staff: configure it carefully, because nothing downstream will catch a mistake. If you point an item at the wrong variant, every redemption of every copy already in members’ hands does the wrong thing, immediately and without asking.

Staff can still move a character afterwards — see Changing a Character’s Rarity — but that is a staff action correcting a mistake, not the item coming back.

2

Staff: setting where it sends a character

On an item type’s edit form, under Administration → Items, there is a Changes variant on use panel. It asks two questions, and they are not the same shape.

  • Becomes — exactly one variant. This is what the character turns into. Because there is only ever one, the member is never asked to choose, and every screen can say plainly what will happen.
  • Spendable on — any number of variants. This is what it works on. “Spendable on a Common or an Uncommon, becomes a Rare” is the shape most communities actually sell.
Ticking nothing under Spendable on covers every variant of the species — including a character with no variant set at all. That is the permissive case, not the broken one, so it is what you get by picking a destination and stopping.
A Common or an Uncommon becomes a Rare. Rare cannot be ticked as a source — an item that turns a Rare into a Rare does nothing, and its holder would only find that out after spending it, so the box is shown disabled rather than hidden.

The species is fixed by the destination. An item cannot move a character between species — a Pillowing cannot become a Lintling, however the variants are named, because the character’s trait values have nothing to do with the other species’ trait list.

Clear the destination to remove the effect entirely.

Only a consumable item type can carry one, for the reason every redeemable item is consumable: redeeming is what uses it up. Without that, one item would move a character every time it was pressed.

Tick Consumable on the item type first.

An item type does one thing when redeemed. A type that already pays out currency, makes characters, or edits traits cannot also change variants — clear the other one first.

3

Seeing what one does before you get one

The item type’s page says what it can be spent on and what it grants. It is public, so somebody weighing up a trade for one can read it without holding it — which matters more here than for the other kinds, because this is the one that cannot be taken back.

Changes variant on use, beside where the other kinds of redeemable item say what they do. The note spells out that there is no review and no way back.
4

Redeeming one

There are two ways in, and they meet at the same page.

From the character. A character you own shows an action naming what it would become, when you actually hold an item that would move it. This is the one most people will use — the character is what you are thinking about.

Both kinds of item offer themselves here when you hold them. The variant one says where the character ends up rather than just that something would change — if you hold items pointing at two different variants it falls back to a general wording, because one button cannot name two destinations.

From the item. In your inventory, one of these carries a Change a variant button, which opens a list of the characters it would move.

Holding more than one groups them; open the group for the per-item buttons.
Only characters it would actually move. A character already at the destination is left off and said so — “you hold nothing for this character” and “you hold one, but this character is already Rare” send a reader looking in completely different places.
Neither link costs anything to follow. The item is spent when you confirm. Open the page, think better of it, close the tab — the item is still yours.
5

The redemption page

There is no variant picker here. The item already decided, so the page states the move rather than offering it. If you hold more than one item that would move this character, you pick which item — and each option names where it sends the character, because different items send it to different places.

The whole page, when nothing needs sorting out first. It is called by the item’s own name, not by anything CharDB invented.
6

When the new variant does not allow a trait

This is the part worth understanding before you sell one of these.

A variant is an allow-list. Each one carries its own set of trait options — the whole reason a Legendary looks different from a Common. So a character can perfectly well be holding a trait value that the variant it is moving to does not offer: a blue-eyed Common moving to a Legendary that only allows amber.

Redeeming does not silently drop those values, and it does not silently keep an impossible one. The page finds each one, names it, and refuses to redeem until you have said what it should become — either a value the new variant does offer, or nothing at all.

The button stays disabled until every listed value is resolved. Only options the new variant actually allows are offered in the replacement dropdown.

Most redemptions never show this panel at all. It appears only when the destination genuinely refuses something the character has, which depends entirely on how your species’ variants are configured — two variants offering the same options will never produce it.

Nothing is spent while this is unresolved. The check happens before the item is destroyed, so a member who opens the page, sees a re-pick they do not like and leaves still has their item. The same check runs again on the server at the moment of the write, so a page left open while staff change the variant’s options cannot slip an impossible value through.
7

The confirm

Redeeming never happens on a single click. The dialog names the item, when it reached you, the character, and what it becomes.

The date is when you got it, from the ledger — not when it was minted. For anything traded those are different, and the one that helps you recognise which item this is is the first.

Cancel and nothing has happened. Confirm and the item is destroyed and the character moved in one write — so a second submitted tab spends one item, not two, and there is no state where the item is gone but the character has not moved.

8

Afterwards: two records

The character’s rarity history gains a line saying what it moved between, who did it, and what bought it. It is on the character page, public, and it is the same history a staff variant change writes to — so a reader can tell a redemption from a staff correction.

“Redeemed Thornwing Ascension” is the difference between a history and a list of mutations.

The community’s item ledger gains a Redeemed row, naming the item, the member, and the reason. Filterable to Redeemed alongside every other kind of redemption.

One row for one redemption, above the grant that put the item in that member’s hands in the first place.

The item’s own provenance page still works after it is gone — being destroyed is an event in its history, not an erasure of it.

9

Every reason a redemption is refused

Wherever possible these are said before the member fills anything in, and the item survives every one of them.

Reason Notes
That character is not yours You can only redeem on characters you own, not on ones you are being offered in a trade.
The character is already that variant Its own message, not folded into “cannot be redeemed on that character” — they send you looking in different places. Such characters are left out of the picker entirely.
The item does not work on that variant Checked against the item’s own source list. An empty list works on all of them.
The item is for a different species An item cannot move a character between species, so it can only ever be spent within its destination’s own.
A trait value the new variant does not allow Re-pick it, or remove it. See step 6 — nothing is spent while it is unresolved.
That character has a change awaiting review An edit kit’s proposal holds trait values chosen for the variant the character is about to leave. Resolve the review first.
That character has no species A character removed from its species has no variants to move between.
The item type is not consumable Redeeming is what uses it up.
You have left the community A species belongs to one community.
The item is not yours, or is already spent Checked at the moment of the write, so a trade landing in between cannot be raced. Submitting the same page twice spends one item.

In every case the item survives.
