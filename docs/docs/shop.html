Overview

A community shop turns currency back into items. Staff list what is for sale and what it costs; members spend what they have earned. Buying grants a real item — the same kind staff grant by hand, with its own row in the item ledger and its own history.

Stock and caps

Limited runs, one per member

Nothing is deducted twice. Stock, coin, and the granted item all move in a single transaction. A purchase that charged and granted nothing, or granted without charging, is worse than one that fails and is tried again — so a shop that runs out mid-checkout takes nothing at all.
1

Listing something for sale

Shop under Administration is where staff define listings. It needs canManageItems — the same permission as creating item types, because a listing is a promise to grant one.

A community that has not listed anything yet.

A listing points at an item type that already exists. Everything else is optional: a display name and description if the shop should say something the item type does not, stock if the run is limited, and a limit per member if one person should not take them all.

Stock and per-member limits are different things. Stock is how many exist to sell. A per-member limit is how many one member may hold at once — and it counts what they have, not how many times they have clicked buy, so undoing a purchase gives the allowance back.
2

Prices: options, and what is in them

A listing has one or more price options. Each option is one complete way to pay, and a buyer picks exactly one. An option can ask for several currencies at once.

Trait Change Potion
Option 1 — 50 Hollow Coin
Option 2 — 20 Hollow Coin and 1 Star Shard

Those are two ways to buy the same potion, not two potions. The editor keeps the distinction in its two buttons: Another way to pay adds an option; and another currency adds a second currency inside the option you are already editing.

One listing, two ways to pay for it. The second option asks for two currencies together.
Both listings. The potion is unlimited but capped at three per member; the locket has a run of two and no cap.
Editing a price never changes what was already paid. The amounts are copied onto the purchase when it happens. Put a listing on sale next week and last week's refunds still return last week's price.
3

The storefront

Members see Shop under Community. Every listing shows its options, what the buyer already holds, and how much of any per-member limit they have used.

The buyer's balance sits in the header, so the prices next to it mean something without a trip to the wallet.

Options a member cannot afford are dimmed, not hidden. A price you cannot pay yet is information — it tells you what to go and earn. Hiding it just makes the shop look smaller than it is.

4

The cart, and confirming

Adding to the cart picks the listing and the option. Two of the same item bought at different options stay on separate lines, because they are genuinely different purchases.

Totals stay per currency. ⬡160 and ✦2 — never added together into a single number, because they are not the same thing.

Buying always asks first, and the confirmation says exactly what will be spent.

The last chance to read the total before any coin moves.
The cart never decides the price. It records which option you picked; the amounts are read from the database at checkout. Affordability shown on the page is advisory — the purchase itself is what decides, so a balance that changed a second ago cannot be spent twice.
Refused at checkout, with nothing taken.
5

What a purchase leaves behind

The coin comes off the balance, the items land in the inventory, and every unit bought becomes its own line under Recent purchases.

Three units bought, three lines. Each one can be undone on its own.

That panel keeps to the eight most recent, and says so — "showing 8 of 23 · view all" — because it sits beside a cart rather than being the history. View all opens Your purchases, which has everything you have bought here, a search by item, and a filter for active against refunded. Anything still inside its fifteen minutes can be undone from there, whether or not the panel had room for it.

Bought items are ordinary items — tradeable, usable, and visible in the item ledger like any other.
6

Undo, for fifteen minutes

A buyer can undo their own purchase for fifteen minutes. The item is taken back, the stock goes up, and the coin returns.

The coin is back, the locket is back on the shelf, and the line is marked refunded rather than removed.

It is deliberately short. This is an undo button for the wrong click, not a returns policy — past the window the item has had time to be traded, used, or shown to somebody. After that a moderator can still refund it, and the page says so instead of leaving the buyer guessing.

Some purchases cannot be undone at all. If the item has been used, destroyed, or traded away, there is nothing to give back. The button explains which of those applies rather than simply failing.

Undoing asks first. Every button that returns coin — the one in the shop's sidebar, the one in your own purchase history, and the staff one below — opens a confirm naming what is being returned. Undoing is not itself reversible: it puts the copy back on a shelf that may be limited, so if somebody takes the last one in the meantime you cannot simply buy it again.

Staff have their own list, under Shop Purchases in the Administration section: everyone's purchases, newest first, with a refund button that is not bound by the fifteen minutes. It needs canGrantItems — the permission that already means "may move items and coin on somebody else's behalf".

The staff refund names the member, the item and the amount — this page lists many people's purchases and the rows look alike. Cancel holds the focus, so a stray keypress refunds nobody.
Refunded lines stay on the list and say who refunded them. A page that hid them could not answer "has someone already dealt with this?"
The reasons staff cannot override are the real ones. The undo window and "this is not your purchase" are the buyer's limits and do not apply here. An item that has been used, destroyed, or traded still cannot come back for anybody — and neither can coin belonging to somebody who has since left the community, so that refund is refused rather than taking the item and giving nothing back.
7

Running out, and hitting the cap

A listing with stock stops selling when it reaches zero. It stays on the page, marked sold out, rather than disappearing — a listing that vanishes looks like it never existed.

Sold out. Refunding one puts it straight back on sale.

A per-member limit works the same way, but only for the member who reached it.

Three of three. Everyone else can still buy it.
The last one goes to one buyer. Two members clicking buy on the final unit at the same moment do not both get it. The database refuses the second decrement outright rather than the shop checking stock and hoping nothing changed in between.
8

What the ledger says afterwards

Every purchase and every refund is a line in the community's currency ledger, readable by any member.

Spends and refunds sit side by side with the grants that funded them.

A refund is a new line, not an edit. The original spend stays exactly where it was and the returned coin appears underneath it. The history reads as two things that happened, which is what did happen — a ledger that quietly rewrote itself would be no use in the argument it exists to settle.

One cart is one spend per currency, not one per item. Buying three things at once reads as a single purchase, because that is how the buyer experienced it.
