Community Currency

Coin your community defines, and a ledger that shows where it came from

0

Overview

A community can define any number of currencies — event coin, shop credit, a token that only spends during one festival. Members earn them, hold them, and send them to each other.

Currency is deliberately not an item type. Items are one row per instance, so each one carries its own history: this potion was granted to you in March and traded twice. Coin is fungible — asking which of your 250 came from a particular prompt is not a question with an answer. So currency gets a balance and a running statement instead.

There is no treasury. Currency is created when staff grant it and it leaves circulation when it is spent or removed. Every unit that exists is in somebody's hands — which is what makes the circulation number on the next page mean something.
1

Creating a currency

Currencies lives under Community in the sidebar, not Administration — the numbers are public, only the actions are restricted.

A community with no currencies yet.

A currency needs a name and a short code. A symbol is optional; the form shows you exactly how amounts will read before you commit.

One checkbox decides whether members can give this to each other. Leave it on for money. Turn it off for something that should be earned rather than acquired — a prompt score, a rank, an event credit — and it can still be granted, spent and refunded, but it cannot be traded or sent to another member. The setting can be changed later either way, and changing it never touches a balance anyone already holds.

With a symbol, amounts read ⬡1,250. Without one, they read 1,250 HC.
Codes are unique per community, and case doesn't help. Trying to add a second currency with the code hc when HC exists is refused — otherwise two currencies would render identically everywhere they appear.
The error names the field you need to change.
2

Granting currency

Granting is how currency comes into existence. Pick an amount, pick everyone who should receive it, and give a reason. Paying eleven people the same amount is one event, not eleven coincidences.

The running total is the guard against a mistyped grant: 250 each to three members is 750 new units of coin.

The reason is public — every member can read it in the ledger. The staff note is not, and is for the candid version.

Who can grant: anyone with canGrantItems. Currency reuses the item permissions rather than adding its own, so whoever hands out prizes already has what they need.
3

Rewarding an upload

Granting from the Currencies page is deliberate and occasional. The other way coin enters circulation is routine: rewarding people for what they post, from inside the moderation queue they are already working through.

A moderator who can also grant currency sees an Award currency panel on each card, listing everyone the upload names.

One pending upload, with the people who could be rewarded for it.
One row per person, not per relationship. Somebody can be several things at once — here the uploader also owns the media and owns the character depicted, so they are one row reading uploader · owner · character owner rather than three. The credited artist is a different person, so they get their own row and their own amount.

Fill in whatever each person should get and press Approve once. The running total is there so a mistyped figure is visible before it is paid, not after.

Different people, different amounts, one action.
Approving and paying are one operation. They commit together or not at all. An approval that quietly failed to pay, or a payment for an upload that never went live, are both worse than the whole thing failing and being retried.

Leaving the amounts blank is normal. An approval with nothing filled in behaves exactly as it did before any of this existed.

Who sees the panel

Only moderators who can also grant currency. Moderating images and handing out rewards are separate permissions, and most moderators hold only the first — for them the queue is unchanged.

The same queue, same card, for a moderator who cannot grant currency. No panel, and nothing else changes.
The panel is hidden by the server, not by the page. It is not sent to anyone who cannot use it, and the approval itself refuses an award from them regardless. Hiding a control is a convenience; it is never the thing keeping anyone out.
4

Earning it by using an item

The third way currency arrives, after a staff grant and an approved upload: a member uses up an item that is worth something. A redemption ticket handed out as an event prize becomes coin when its holder decides to spend it, without staff doing anything at the moment it happens.

Staff set what a type pays on the item type itself, so the decision is made once rather than per member. The item is destroyed in the same breath as the payment — that is what stops the same ticket being redeemed twice — and both halves carry one identifier, so the coin can be traced back to the item it came from.

It reads as an ordinary ledger entry, with a way back to the item. The full walkthrough is in Item Ledger & Provenance.

Reading the supply table

The table is the point of the page. Before granting five thousand more of something, you should be able to see how much already exists without navigating anywhere.

750 granted less 250 removed leaves 500 in circulation. The arithmetic is visible, which is the whole idea.
Column What it counts
In circulation Every unit held by anybody, right now.
Holders People with a non-zero balance. Not the same as circulation — 500 held by two people is 500 in circulation and 2 holders.
Granted 30d Created over the last thirty days.
Removed 30d Taken back or spent over the same window. Transfers are not counted: moving coin between members changes nothing about how much exists.
Largest The biggest single balance. A number well clear of the others is worth a look.

Any member can read this page. They simply get no buttons.

The same numbers, seen by an ordinary member. No Grant, no Remove, no New currency.

A member's wallet

Currency sits above items on the inventory page rather than on a page of its own. Coin and items are one answer to "what does this person have", and splitting them would make a trade partner check two places.

Both formatting styles side by side: ⬡190 for a currency with a symbol, 40 LM for one without.
Currencies you hold none of are still listed. A wallet showing only what you already have cannot tell you a currency exists — which is the one thing you need to know before you can go and earn any.
7

Sending currency to another member

Members send their own coin without staff involvement. There is no permission for it: the balance is the authorisation — you cannot send what you do not hold.

The hint shows what you hold, so you are not doing the subtraction in your head.

Balances never go negative. An attempt to send or remove more than someone holds is refused outright, and nothing moves — the recipient is not credited from a balance that could not fund it.

Staff trying to remove more than a member holds gets the same treatment.
Some currencies cannot be sent at all. A currency can be marked untradeable, and then it moves in one direction only: staff grant it, members spend it in the shop, staff can take it back — but it never passes from one member to another, by send or by trade. Its wallet card says so where the Send button would be, rather than quietly having no button on a wallet where every other card does. This is not archiving: an untradeable currency is fully alive, and staff work with it exactly as before.

Viewing someone else's wallet shows their balances and no Send buttons. Balances are public within a community for the same reason item provenance is: you should be able to check a trade partner before the trade, not after.

Another member's wallet. The dimmed card is a currency they hold none of.
8

The currency ledger

Every movement of currency in the community, newest first, filterable by kind and by currency, and searchable by reason.

The ledger as staff see it. The boxed lines beneath a reason are private staff notes.
Kind What happened
Granted Staff created coin into a member's balance.
Transfer One member sent coin to another.
Spent A member spent it. The coin leaves circulation rather than going anywhere.
Removed Staff took coin back, as a correction or a penalty.
Imported A balance that predates the ledger.

A row created by an approval says so, and links back to the upload it paid for.

The reward from the previous step, as both members will see it.
Where the money came from is part of the record. The reason line says why in words; the link underneath says what, and goes there. Without it a member reading “+25, upload approved” has no way to tell which upload, and staff looking back over forty approvals cannot tell the rows apart at all.
A transfer is one line, not two. Underneath, it is recorded as two entries — a debit and a credit — so that each member's own statement reads correctly from their side. In the community view both halves are visible at once, so they are shown as the single movement they are.

A bulk grant is not collapsed the same way. Each recipient genuinely received their own coin, and rolling eleven people into one line would hide who was paid.

What a member sees instead

The ledger is readable by any member of the community. Staff notes are the one exception, and the difference is worth seeing directly. Compare this with the screenshot in the previous step — same page, same events, same order.

Same rows, same reasons, no staff notes.
The notes are not merely hidden by the page. The server does not send them to a viewer without item permissions, and they are excluded from search — so a member cannot probe for the contents of a note they cannot read, one guess at a time.
10

Retiring a currency

Currencies are archived, never deleted. An archived currency stops accepting new transactions, but every balance and every statement line stays readable.

Why not just delete it? Deleting a currency would destroy the history of everything anyone ever bought with it. A year-old dispute about a trade is exactly the case where that history matters, and exactly the case where the currency involved is most likely to be retired.

An archived currency still appears in the supply table — coin already in members' hands does not vanish, so leaving it out would understate what exists. It disappears from wallets and from the grant flow, and can be restored at any time.
