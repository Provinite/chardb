Trading

Two members, one table, and nothing moving until both agree

0

Overview

A trade is an offer one member makes to another inside a community: some of your items and some of your coin, for some of theirs. You build it, they answer it. Until they answer, nothing has happened.

Both sides of a trade can carry items, currency, or both, and either side can be empty — a gift is just a trade that asks for nothing back.

🧾

Compose

Pick what you give and what you want

📥

Answer

Accept, decline, or counter

🔁

Settle

Items and coin move together, or not at all

Nothing is escrowed. An open offer does not take anything off you or freeze your inventory — you keep everything until the moment it settles. That is deliberate: the alternative is a member whose items are locked up in an offer the other party forgot about. The cost is that an offer can go stale, which is why every condition is checked again at accept rather than trusted from when it was written.
1

Building an offer

Start from a member's row on the community's Members page. The composer has three panes: what you hold on the left, what they hold on the right, and the table you are building in the middle.

One potion and 75 coin, for one of their charms.

The two panes are not symmetrical, and that is the point. Your side lists every copy separately, because you are here and can choose which one leaves. Their side lists one line per item type with a count, because any copy will do — pinning one of theirs would make the offer fail the moment they traded that particular copy to someone else.

An item type its community has marked untradeable shows as locked rather than being hidden, so you can see it is there and see why it will not move. Your own untradeable holdings collapse to a single locked line with a count; there is no point listing thirty of them one by one when none can be picked.

Coin is a field on each side of the table rather than a line on it, because it is a price rather than a thing. Put an amount on both sides and only the difference is sent: 250 out and 100 back becomes 150 one way. The currency picker only offers currencies you actually hold, and never an archived one — archived currencies keep their balances and stay readable but refuse new transactions.

2

One item, one open offer

You cannot put the same item on the table in two open offers at once. Try it and the offer is refused with the item named.

Refused at send, naming the item, while you can still pick a different one.
Why not just let both stand? Because only one of them could ever settle. The second person to accept would hit a failure they had no way to see coming and no way to fix — the item was gone before they clicked. Refusing at compose time puts the problem in front of the only person who can do anything about it.

This applies to your specific items. Asking two different people for one of their charms is fine, because you are asking for a type rather than a copy, and either of them can still fill it.

Coin works the same way. What you can put on the table is your balance minus whatever your other open offers have already promised, so 300 of a 380 balance in one offer leaves 80 for the next — and the refusal says so, because "you do not have 200" is baffling to someone looking at 380.

3

Your trades

Trades in the sidebar is one list holding both directions: offers waiting on you and offers you have out. Each row says which way it points in its own words, so there is no second place to check before you know whether anything needs you.

Open, Settled and All. The chip under the heading narrows the list to one community; the × clears it.

Reaching it from a community's sidebar narrows it to that community. Reaching it from the global sidebar does not, because an offer waiting on you is waiting on you wherever it was made.

4

Answering one

An offer reads from your side: what you give at the top, what you receive below it, and the sender's note underneath.

The same trade the other member built, told the other way round.

These are the states an offer can be in:

State Means
Awaiting an answer Open. The recipient can accept, decline or counter; the sender can withdraw
Settled Accepted, and everything on the table has moved
Declined The recipient said no, or countered
Withdrawn The sender took it back before it was answered
Expired Nobody answered within seven days
Expiry is a deadline, not an event. An offer carries a date, and it is judged against the clock whenever anyone looks at it — including at the moment someone tries to accept. Nothing has to run on a schedule to sweep old offers, and there is no window in which an offer reads as expired but can still be accepted.

Accepting sends no choice about which of their copies you get. A by-type line means any of them will do, so the giver's side picks — newest first — rather than asking you a question you have no basis to answer.

5

Countering

Counter… opens the composer on the same table with you as the sender, ready to edit. Sending it declines the offer you were answering and delivers yours in a single step.

Their offer, carried over. Change the coin, swap an item, send it back — or leave, and theirs is still there.

What was coming to you becomes what you ask for, what was being asked of you becomes rows picked from your own holdings, and the coin flips direction. Anything they have traded away since they wrote the offer is quietly dropped rather than carried over into a request that could not be filled.

Opening a counter costs you nothing. Their offer stands, and stays acceptable, right up until you send yours — so you can open the composer, see what a counter would look like, and walk away with the original still on the table. What you cannot do is have both: sending the counter declines theirs, and there is no way back to it afterwards.
6

Settlement

Accepting moves everything at once: every item on both sides and the coin, in a single step that either completes or does nothing.

Settled. The expiry line is gone, and so are the buttons.

Because nothing was held while the offer stood, every condition is checked again at this moment rather than trusted from when the offer was written: that both of you still hold what you promised, that the payer can cover the coin, that the item types are still tradeable, and that the offer has not expired. If any of that has changed, the trade fails and says which part — and nothing moves.

An offer that fails at accept is not a bug. It is the honest outcome of an offer that has gone stale, and the message names the line that no longer holds. The alternative — settling anyway — would move items their owner had already promised elsewhere.
7

What it leaves behind

A settled trade writes to both community ledgers, which any member can read. This is what makes a traded item's history something you can check rather than something you have to take on trust.

One line per leg. A trade moves two different things in two different directions, so it is two lines rather than one.
The coin leg, in the currency ledger, under the same reason.

Both legs of one settlement share an identifier, so the item movement and the coin movement can be recognised as parts of the same event rather than two things that happened to occur together.

Declining, withdrawing and expiring write nothing to either ledger. Nothing was held while the offer stood, so there is nothing to release — and a ledger recording every offer that was never taken up would bury the movements that actually happened.
